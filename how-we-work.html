The KSC milestone model

The financing round is sized to the milestone it funds. Not to a runway estimate.

Pre-Commercialisation Milestones

Four questions to the next milestone. Then the next one is mapped.

At each milestone we ask each question again, until the device is in clinical use.

01

What stage is my company?

We evaluate the company against what funders at its next milestone will require, not against the end point. Investors ask for more evidence when they do not yet understand the market or the company.

02

What does this milestone accomplish?

With the company, we define what the milestone has to prove. It reduces investor risk and makes the company more valuable. Funding is sized to complete the milestone.

03

Which funders fit?

Family offices, grants, angel groups and venture capital, or a mix: who they are and what motivates them to invest. We read the company for the stage it has reached, not by the name of its round.

04

Who does the work, and when?

We select and qualify the specialists, in the UK and the US. They work to the company's plan alongside its other advisers. When the milestone is reached, the specialists may change. It's in the plan.

Commercial Milestones

A CE mark or a 510(k) gets you to the start line. Seven more decisions get you to scale.

Once the product is cleared, the regulator stops being the gatekeeper and the hospital takes over. That means a clinician who has to want it, a committee that has to approve it and a budget holder who has to pay for it. We work through the seven decisions with the company and its team, in order.

05

Who uses it first?

The first ten centres. The right clinician, not the biggest site. A champion with time, interest and a waiting list matters more than bed count.

06

Where do the patients come from?

Pathways, not opinions. Patients reach a specialist through a referral route. If the route is not mapped, the diary stays empty.

07

Who approves the purchase?

New product committees, procurement and finance. Each wants a different document, and each meets on its own calendar.

08

Who pays?

In the UK, a named budget, tariff or commissioner. In the US it is private insurance, Medicare or self-pay. Each one changes the price, the evidence and the sales cycle. Our team has experience in the UK, the US and the EU.

09

How does it reach the hospital?

Direct, distributor or framework. Each suits a different product and price point. The terms change by market, but the process is the same in the UK and across the US.

10

Who sells it?

The first hires. One or two people who can build, not just sell. The first hire opens doors with no reference sites. That is a different person from the one who grows an established patch.

11

What keeps them buying?

Real-world results validate adoption. Audit data, service evaluations and health technology assessment work turn early sites into references.

Each decision has an order. Getting it wrong costs a year.
